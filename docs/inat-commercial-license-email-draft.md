Hi iNaturalist team,

I'm Alexa — an avid hiker and amateur wildlife photographer, and the solo creator
of US Wildlife Explorer (https://wildlifeexplorer.us), a free map that shows the
wildlife at 4,700+ US parks (national parks, all 50 states' state parks, and the
National Wildlife Refuges) and the best season to spot each species.

iNaturalist is how the app knows the mammals, reptiles, amphibians, fish, and
insects for each area — I use your API to pull the research-grade species
documented in a park's county, so a visitor sees the animals they can actually
expect. I attribute iNaturalist on every park view, keep request volume well under
your rate limits with heavy caching, and I do **not** train any AI/ML model on
iNaturalist data. Where I show observation media, I respect each observer's
individual license. The site is completely free — no paywall, no accounts.

As traffic grows, hosting and map costs do too, and I'm hoping to help cover them —
mainly through reader donations, and possibly some light, unobtrusive advertising
down the road. Because your terms restrict commercial use, I wanted to ask first
rather than assume.

Would iNaturalist be open to permitting that for a small, free, hobbyist project
like mine? I'm glad to share traffic numbers, exactly how the data is used, or
anything else that helps. And thank you — iNaturalist made the non-bird half of
this app possible.

Best,

### Notes (context, not the email)
- **Two separate license layers on iNat:** (1) the observation *records* (species,
  place, date) — governed by iNat's terms + each record's CC license; (2) the
  *photos* — licensed individually by each photographer, and MANY are CC-BY-NC
  (non-commercial). Even with iNat's blessing, CC-BY-NC photos can't be used
  commercially. The app's non-bird floors use occurrence *data/counts*, not photos,
  so this is mostly about the data layer — but if a paid/ad tier ever shows iNat
  photos, those must be filtered to commercial-OK licenses (CC0 / CC-BY) or dropped.
- **No AI/ML training:** iNat's terms specifically forbid training models on their
  data for commercial purposes. The app doesn't do this — keep it that way.
- Donations alone are non-commercial and don't need this — independent of iNat's answer.
