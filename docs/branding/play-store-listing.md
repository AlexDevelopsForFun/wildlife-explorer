## Full description (4,000 char max)

```
Ever arrived at a park and wondered what you might actually see?

US Wildlife Explorer answers that for more than 4,700 parks across the United States — every national park, all 50 states' state parks, and 543 National Wildlife Refuges. Open any park and you'll see the animals documented there, how likely you are to encounter each one, and the season you're most likely to spot it.

WHAT YOU CAN DO

🔎 Search any animal
Type "Bald Eagle", "Black Bear" or "Monarch" and see every park where it's been recorded. The fastest way to plan a trip around a species you want to find.

📍 Find parks near you
Tap "Near me" for the closest national parks, state parks and refuges sorted by distance — plus which rare birds have turned up in the area recently.

🎚️ Filter for your actual trip
Set the season and likelihood filters to answer "what will I really see in July?" Sort by Most Iconic to put the bears, eagles and foxes first.

✅ Keep a life list
Mark species as seen to build a personal life list that remembers everything you've spotted, across every park you visit.

🥾 Plan the visit
Directions and hiking trails for every park, plus a photo of what it actually looks like.

📶 Works offline
Park data is saved to your device, so it keeps working deep in the backcountry where there's no signal.

WHERE THE DATA COMES FROM

Sightings come from real scientific records, not guesswork:

• eBird (Cornell Lab of Ornithology) — bird observations and how often each species is reported
• iNaturalist — research-grade mammal, reptile, amphibian, fish and insect records
• National Park Service — official park information

Every species shows an honest likelihood — Guaranteed, Very Likely, Likely, Unlikely or Rare — based on how often it has actually been reported there. When sightings right at a spot are sparse, the app tells you so and shows the wildlife documented in the surrounding county instead, rather than pretending to know more than it does.

FREE, NO ADS, NO SIGN-UP

No account, no paywall, no advertising. Your life list stays on your device. Location is used only if you tap "Near me", and it's never stored.

Built by one person who kept wishing this existed on the trail.
```

*(~2,050 characters — well inside the 4,000 limit)*
