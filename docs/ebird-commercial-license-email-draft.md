# eBird permission email — personal version (ready to send)

**To:** ebird@cornell.edu  *(the exact address §2d of the API Terms names for permission requests)*
**Subject:** Permission to use the eBird API on a free wildlife app (wildlifeexplorer.us)

---

Hi there,

I'm Alexa — an avid hiker and amateur wildlife photographer, and the solo creator
of US Wildlife Explorer (https://wildlifeexplorer.us).

I built it to answer a question I kept having on the trail: when I get to a new
park, what animals might I actually see, how likely am I to see them, and when's
the best time to go? I couldn't find that anywhere in one place, so I made it —
a free map that shows the wildlife at 4,700+ US parks (the national parks, all 50
states' state parks, and the National Wildlife Refuges) and the best season to
spot each species.

eBird is the backbone of the bird data, and I'm a big fan of the Lab's work. I
attribute eBird with a link back to eBird.org on every park view, my API key stays
server-side, and I keep request volume low with caching — I've tried to be a good
guest. The site is completely free, with no paywall and no accounts.

I'd love to keep it running as it grows. Hosting and map costs go up with traffic,
so I'm hoping to help cover them — mainly through reader donations, and possibly
some light, unobtrusive advertising down the road. Since your terms ask for written
permission before any advertising (§2d), I wanted to reach out and ask first rather
than just flip it on.

Would the Lab be open to permitting that kind of use for a small, free, hobbyist
project like mine? I'm happy to share traffic numbers, exactly how the data is
used, or anything else that would help.

Thank you for eBird — it genuinely made this whole thing possible.

Best,
Alexa
wildlifeexplorer.us · contact@wildlifeexplorer.us

---

### Before you send
- **To: ebird@cornell.edu** — the address the terms themselves name.
- **Send from an email you'll keep** (your personal Gmail is fine; the signature
  gives them contact@wildlifeexplorer.us as the project address).
- It's ~250 words and deliberately personal — that's the point. Don't make it more
  formal; the human, hobbyist tone is what makes a small request like this land.

### Notes (context, not the email)
- This asks open-endedly — it does NOT promise "no profit" or commit you to
  anything binding. It's just an honest request.
- A second linked doc, "eBird Data Access Terms of Use," also applies — worth a
  skim, but the API Terms are what govern this and name ebird@cornell.edu.
- Donations alone are generally non-commercial and don't need this — your Buy Me a
  Coffee page can go up now regardless of eBird's answer.
